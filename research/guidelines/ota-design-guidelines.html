Ōta design guidelines

The rules for the redesigned Ōta app: a pastel, mascot-led fan app for anime lovers in India, with one progression stat, Ōra, behind everything. Use this page to build any new screen so it looks, moves and rewards like the rest.

Interactive prototype Systems blueprint Old app teardown Style explorations
Principles

Five rules behind every screen

One stat, one storyEverything you do earns Ōra. Rank, perks and spending all read from it. Never add a second points currency.
Every screen has a hostDiscover is TAKU's, Battle is Puri's, Stage is Momo's. The host greets, reacts and fills empty states.
Rewards you can feelEvery gain animates: a +number floats, the chip ticks up, the mascot reacts. No silent rewards.
Pastel, not childishCartoon lives in the mascots only. Cards, type and controls are clean, modern and grown-up.
Fair by designOdds are always visible, pity is shown, gacha never takes real money, progress bars always mean something.

Do

  • Use a mascot's pastel as the background of anything that mascot hosts.
  • Keep violet for Ōra so people learn "violet = power".
  • Use lime for one thing per screen: the current state or the success.

Don't

  • Put text in a mascot's pastel colour; use its deep tone.
  • Use rarity colours outside gacha, prizes and tickets.
  • Add gradients to surfaces. Only capsules (two-tone) and the holo shine use them.

Shape, space and depth

Radius

  • 999 buttons, chips, tab bar
  • 28–32 big cards, stage panels, sheets
  • 20–24 list rows, option cards
  • 14–16 mascot tiles, inputs
  • 8–10 tiny bars and swatches

Spacing (4-pt)

  • Screen gutter 16
  • Between cards 12–14
  • Inside cards 14–20
  • Row padding 7–12
  • Touch targets ≥ 44, main CTAs 56–60 tall

Depth

  • Cards are flat white on lilac: no borders, no shadows.
  • Shadows only for things that float: sheets, the tab bar, prize cards, speech bubbles.
  • Soft shadow: 0 10px 28px rgba(29,26,43,.08)
  • Prize glow uses the rarity colour.

Motion

UI motion is quick and springy. Mascot motion is squash-and-stretch from the feet. Celebrations are big but rare, and there is only ever one at a time.

Rules

  • Every reward moves to where it is stored: Ōra to the chip, tickets to the ticket count.
  • Never block input for more than 1 s, except the skippable cutscene.
  • One-shot reactions return to idle on their own.
  • Bars fill over 0.8–0.9 s so people see the progress happen.

Reduced motion

  • Loops stop and mascots hold their resting pose.
  • Pops and sheets become instant fades.
  • The boss fight becomes a short sequence of still comic panels.
  • Haptics (light / medium / success) still play on device.

Game layer

Progression: Ōra

Ōra replaces points, coins, XP and rank score. It has two numbers. Power is everything you have ever earned and sets your rank. Wallet is what you can spend. Spending never lowers your rank.

Multipliers and daily items

  • Streak (one Daily Challenge a day): ×1.1 at 3 days, ×1.2 at 7, ×1.3 at 30, ×1.5 at 100
  • Cheers: 3 a day, rising to 6 at Kami; expire at midnight
  • Stars: 3 per rank; each star-up gives a partner toast and a Standard ticket
  • A typical engaged day earns about 600–750 Ōra

Rank-up boss fight

0.0 s

Overload

The Ōra bar overflows, the screen shakes, "ŌRA OVERLOAD".

1.3 s

Boss drops

The gate boss slams down with its name card and a line.

3.0 s

Team-up

TAKU and Puri charge in from the sides with attack streaks.

4.7 s

Finisher

Your partner fires the beam (Momo's Encore Beam).

5.6 s

Poof

The boss flashes and bursts into puffs.

6.7 s

Spoils

The new frame spins onto your avatar; perks and a Rare+ ticket.

The fight always ends in a win. It waits until you finish a quiz or checkout, can be skipped after the first beat, and can be replayed from the journey map.

Fairness rules

  • Odds show next to the Pull button on every tier.
  • No real-money pulls. Tickets come from play or Ōra only.
  • Duplicates convert to Ōra: 20 / 60 / 150 / 500.
  • The empty state never dead-ends: it offers an Ōra pull or a button to the next ticket quest.

Do

  • Anchor all motion at the feet (origin 50% 90%).
  • Let one mascot speak per screen; the trio together only for onboarding, rank-ups, Legendary pulls and a cleared quest board.
  • Give every empty state its host mascot plus one button that fixes it.

Don't

  • Put mascots on every card or list row; small tiles in quest rows are the limit.
  • Make a mascot sad because the user did something wrong on purpose (like skipping).
  • Use mascot speech for legal, payment or error detail. Say those plainly in the UI.

Writing

  • UI copy is plain and active: "Turn the handle", "Pay & book", "Pull again".
  • Mascot lines are short, in character, and never carry rules or prices.
  • Rewards always say what and how much: "+150 Ōra and a ticket".
  • Disabled states say how to unlock: "Earn at event check-in".
  • Japanese words: kana or kanji, romaji, then meaning.
  • Hindi and English mixing is fine in mascot lines once localisation starts.

Accessibility

  • Text contrast 4.5:1 or better; mascot deep tones are tuned for this on white and pastel.
  • Touch targets 44 px minimum; main actions 56–60 px.
  • Every mascot image has alt text; decorative bursts and sparkles are hidden.
  • Rarity is never colour alone: the label is always written.
  • Reduced motion honoured everywhere (see Motion).
  • Haptics: light on taps, medium on capsule cracks, success on rewards.
